The Questions Your Med Spa Provider Will Ask You

At your first visit, your provider is mostly trying to learn four things: what you want to change, your medical history, your lifestyle, and what you put on your skin now. Those answers steer everything that happens next. Clear, honest details help them suggest treatments that actually fit you and avoid ones that could waste your money or cause a problem.

Your Goals: What Do You Want to Fix?

The starting point is what you see in the mirror. When they ask, "What brings you in today?" try to go past general answers. Instead of "I want to look less tired," say what makes you feel that way. Dark circles? Lines at the corners of your eyes? Dull, uneven tone? Point to the spots if that helps.

They may ask you to show the exact areas that bug you. They want to see things through your eyes. A crease you think about every morning might barely register to them, but your view is what matters here. That talk helps them match your concerns to real options. If sun spots bother you, they might talk about a laser. If your forehead lines are the main issue, they might bring up Botox and similar injectables. This is where you start building a plan together.

Medical History and Past Treatments

This part keeps you safe. Your provider needs a full picture of your health to lower your risk of side effects. Expect questions about:

Allergies, especially to medications or numbing agents.

Any chronic conditions, like autoimmune disorders, rosacea, or a history of cold sores.

Any medications you take regularly, including over the counter pills, vitamins, and supplements. Things like aspirin, ibuprofen, fish oil, and vitamin E can raise your chance of bruising.

If you have ever taken Accutane, and when.

Your history with cosmetic treatments. Have you had Botox, filler, or laser before? When was your last treatment? Did you like the results? Any reactions?

Be honest, even if something feels minor or embarrassing. For example, if you tend to get cold sores, some lasers or microneedling can trigger a flare if you do not take preventive medication. If you want something for fine lines, a Los Angeles provider like the one at skinanthology.com/injectables will ask about past injectables and how you healed. Leaving out a bad reaction does not protect you. It just makes a repeat more likely.

Lifestyle and Your Daily Routine

Your day to day life affects how your skin behaves and what is realistic for you. Your provider will usually ask about sun. Do you work outdoors? Use tanning beds? Wear sunscreen most days? Someone who is in strong sun a lot may not be right for certain stronger lasers, because the risk of pigment changes is higher. For more detail on safe sun habits, the Skin Cancer Foundation has clear guides.

They also need to know what kind of downtime you can handle. Can you stay home while you are red and peeling for a week, or do you need something light that lets you go right back to work? Try to answer in a way that matches your actual life, not what sounds ideal. If you say a week of social downtime is fine but feel upset when you cannot go to a party two days later, that mismatch is stressful.

Your current skincare routine matters too. Bring a list or photos of your products. They need to know if you use stronger ingredients like retinoids or acids. You will usually stop those for a bit before and after certain treatments to keep irritation down.

Budget and Expectations

Money can feel awkward to bring up, but it helps your provider plan. Some will ask what you are comfortable spending. That is so they can suggest options that fit, not to stretch you. If a series of pricier laser sessions is not realistic, it is better to say that so they can talk about slower, lower cost options.

They will also walk you through what a treatment can and cannot do. A good provider sets limits and gives you a clear picture of possible results, how many sessions you might need, and how long those results usually last. This is also when you talk about timing. If you are aligned on goals and plan, some people choose to start a treatment that day during their first visit.
