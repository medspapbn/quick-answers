What to Do If You Dislike Your Med Spa Results

If you are not happy with your med spa results, contact the clinic where you had the treatment before you do anything else. Most early worries come from swelling, bruising, or just needing more time for things to settle. A solid provider wants to know if you are concerned and will bring you in for a follow-up to see what is going on. Jumping straight into panic or running to another clinic right away can make things harder to fix. Start with a clear, calm conversation with the original provider.

First, Understand Healing and Timelines

A lot of disappointment comes from not knowing the normal timeline. The face you see in the mirror a day or two after your appointment is not the final result. Almost every treatment has a phase where things look worse or just odd before they look better.

For example, Botox and other neuromodulators can take up to two weeks to show their full effect. Dermal fillers often cause swelling and sometimes bruising that can hide the true outcome for a week or more. Laser treatments and chemical peels can leave your skin red, flaky, and sensitive. The real result shows only after your skin has fully recovered. Microneedling results build over weeks as your body makes new collagen.

Before you decide you do not like the outcome, give it the full healing window. Your provider should have explained the process and timing during your consultation. Read your aftercare instructions again and compare them to what you see. If what you are seeing matches what they described for this phase, wait it out.

When and How to Contact Your Provider

Knowing when to call matters. Some situations need an immediate call, and some can wait for a planned check-in.

You should call your provider right away if you experience:

  • Signs of an infection, like fever, pus, or heat at the injection site.
  • Severe or worsening pain.
  • Unusual skin discoloration that looks like a blocked blood vessel.

For appearance issues, it is usually best to wait until the first healing phase has passed. If you feel a Botox treatment is uneven, wait the full two weeks before asking for an adjustment. If you think your filler is lumpy or asymmetrical, wait for the swelling to fully go down. When you call, say clearly that you would like a follow-up to review your results. Most solid clinics build these follow-ups into their routine.

Having a Productive Follow-Up Appointment

At your follow-up, the goal is to figure out what can be improved. Being specific helps a lot. "I just hate it" does not give your provider much to work with. Try to point to exact things, like, "This side of my forehead seems to move more than the other," or, "I notice a small bump here now that the swelling is gone."

Taking photos in good, consistent lighting before your treatment and during the healing period can help you and your provider compare what changed.

Most providers want you to be satisfied, but they need clear feedback. A calm, detailed talk is much more likely to lead to a fix that makes sense. They know the treatment they did and usually are in the best position to adjust it safely.

Common Fixes and Adjustments

Most cosmetic concerns after treatment have some kind of next step. A thoughtful provider will walk you through what is realistic.

With neuromodulators like Botox, a small touch-up can often correct asymmetry or soften a line that was missed. This is usually a quick office visit.

For hyaluronic acid fillers, adjustments are also possible. If an area needs more volume, a little more product can be added. If an area looks overdone or a lump does not settle, the filler can be dissolved with an enzyme called hyaluronidase. That is a separate procedure, but it is a reliable way to back things down.

If you are underwhelmed by a skin treatment, the talk may shift to your long-term plan. You might need more sessions than you first expected, or a different treatment might match your skin better. Choosing between options like a gentler resurfacing choice often comes down to your goals, and sometimes the first plan needs to be revised. A careful provider in Los Angeles, such as the team at Skin Anthology in Los Angeles, will walk through what to expect from a procedure like microneedling and set realistic timelines from the start.

When a Second Opinion Makes Sense

If you have had a follow-up and are still not satisfied, it is fair to think about a second opinion. Sometimes your style and your provider's style just do not match, even if nothing was done wrong medically.

If you see someone new, they need your full treatment record, including what product was used, how much, and where it went. Do not rush into a "correction" with a new person who does not have those details. Start with a real consultation. If you think you were treated improperly or have a serious complication that your original provider is brushing off, you should see another qualified provider for an in-person assessment.

Preventing Disappointment in the First Place

The easiest way to deal with a bad result is to lower the odds of getting one. That starts with who you choose. Look for a licensed, experienced provider whose before and after photos look natural and match what you like.

The consultation is where you set the tone. Use that time to explain what you want and listen closely when the provider tells you what is realistic. Be careful if someone guarantees perfection or makes promises that sound too good. A solid provider will talk through possible downsides and limits along with benefits. They should also care about your overall skin health and bring up sun protection. The Skin Cancer Foundation has good basic information if you want to read more about that. Clear planning and honest communication before your treatment give you the best chance at a result you feel good about.
