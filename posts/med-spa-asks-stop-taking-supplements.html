Why Your Med Spa Asks You to Stop Taking Supplements

Providers ask you to pause certain supplements and vitamins before a treatment because some of them change how your body clots and heals. Healthy, over the counter products can still raise your risk of bleeding, bruising, swelling, or a stronger reaction than expected. Taking a short break lowers those risks and helps your treatment stay routine.

The Main Concern: Bleeding and Bruising

The main worry is that many supplements thin your blood or affect clotting. That might not matter much day to day, but it does matter when a treatment uses needles or breaks the skin, even a little. Filler injections, Botox, and microneedling all fall into that group. If your blood is thinner, you are more likely to bleed during the visit and bruise afterward.

Extra bleeding can make it harder for your provider to see what they are doing while they work. The bruising can also be darker and last longer, which means more time before you feel comfortable going back to normal plans. This is not about how skilled the injector is. It is just how your body responds when clotting is dialed down.

Some of the most common supplements that do this include:

  • Fish oil and other omega-3 fatty acids
  • High-dose Vitamin E
  • Ginkgo biloba
  • Garlic
  • Ginseng
  • Ginger
  • Turmeric or curcumin

This is not a full list, but it hits the ones that come up most often. Your clinic should give you their own list and clear timing for when to stop each one.

Sun Sensitivity and Laser Treatments

Another concern is photosensitivity, which means your skin reacts more strongly to light. Certain herbs and supplements can do this. That matters if you are booked for light or laser based work, like IPL for dark spots or laser hair removal.

St. John's Wort is a classic example of something that can increase sun sensitivity. If you are taking it and then have a laser session, your skin can overreact. That can raise the chance of burns, blisters, or pigment changes afterward. The point of treatment is to improve your skin, not trade one concern for another.

Good sunscreen and shade are always important after any skin treatment, but avoiding photosensitizing supplements before you go in adds another layer of safety. For practical details on sun protection in general, the Skin Cancer Foundation has solid basics you can read through.

What to Disclose at Your Consultation

Tell your provider about everything you take. All of it matters here, not just prescriptions. Writing it down before your appointment so you are not trying to remember on the spot really helps.

Include everything:

  • Prescription medications
  • Vitamins and mineral supplements
  • Herbal remedies
  • Dietary supplements like fish oil or protein powders
  • Even certain teas you drink regularly, like green tea

A standard multivitamin is often fine, since the doses are usually low. Higher-dose, single-ingredient supplements are more likely to be an issue. But do not try to sort this out by yourself. Give your full list, and let your provider decide what needs a pause and what does not.

Your health history is not something you can get wrong. It is information your provider needs so they can keep you safe and plan a treatment that makes sense for your body.

When to Stop and When to Start Again

Timing is usually simple. Most clinics will tell you to stop specific supplements about one to two weeks before your visit. That gives your body time to clear the blood-thinning effects so your clotting returns to its usual level and bruising risk goes down.

You can often restart your supplements a few days after your treatment. The exact timing depends on what you had done and how your skin looks afterward. With injectables, your provider might ask you to wait until any bruising or tenderness has mostly settled. Follow the aftercare instructions they give you, even if they feel very basic.

Being careful with both your prep and your aftercare is a real part of getting good results. It also tells you something about the clinic you chose. Clear, specific instructions are one of the details to ask about when you are deciding where to go. If you are unsure about any supplement or medication you use, bring it up directly with a licensed provider so you are not guessing.
