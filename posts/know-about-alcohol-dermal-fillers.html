What to Know About Alcohol and Dermal Fillers

No. It is better not to drink alcohol for a day or two after dermal fillers. That post-appointment glass of wine sounds fun, but it can make bruising and swelling worse. It will not ruin your results, but it can make the healing phase more obvious than it needs to be.

Why Alcohol and Fillers Don't Mix

Alcohol widens your blood vessels, so more blood flows through them, including in your face. After filler, you have tiny needle entry points in the skin that are trying to close up and heal.

When blood flow increases, a bit more blood can leak under the skin and show up as a bruise. Even the best injector can nick a small vessel, and if your circulation is revved up, that tiny spot can look like a bigger mark. Extra blood flow also means more fluid in the area, so swelling can look and feel worse.

Alcohol also dries you out. Skin heals better when you are well hydrated. If you are dehydrated, your skin can look dull and recovery can drag. Plain water is a better choice while your skin and filler are settling.

The Same Goes for Before Your Appointment

The no-alcohol advice also applies to the 24 to 48 hours before fillers. Showing up after a night of drinking sets you up for more bruising and swelling from the start. The vasodilation effect is already in play when the needle goes in.

This can cause more pinpoint bleeding during the injections. It is not dangerous, but it can make it a bit harder for your provider to see as clearly and can bump up the chances of a bruise forming right away. Skipping alcohol beforehand gives your injector a cleaner canvas and usually means an easier recovery.

Your provider will likely give you a list of other things to avoid before treatment, often including medications and supplements that thin the blood, like aspirin, ibuprofen, fish oil, and vitamin E. Avoiding these, along with alcohol, is standard if you want less bruising.

What Happens If You Forget and Have a Drink?

Do not panic. One drink will not dissolve your filler or wreck your results. If you had a glass of wine with dinner and remembered later, you have not caused permanent harm. The effects are almost always short term and cosmetic.

The filler will be fine. Your social calendar for the next few days might just be a little bruised.

You are just more likely to see some purple or blue marks at the injection sites. You may also feel puffier the next morning. Bruising and swelling can take an extra day or two to settle. It is annoying, not serious. Go back to drinking water and stick to the rest of your aftercare plan.

A Simple Timeline for Best Results

The timing is simple. Give yourself a small no-alcohol buffer around your appointment so your body can heal without extra hurdles. A common rule is about 48 hours on each side.

  • Before Fillers: Stop drinking alcohol at least 24 to 48 hours before your scheduled appointment time.
  • After Fillers: Wait at least 24 to 48 hours after your treatment before having any alcoholic beverages.

This waiting period lets the early swelling calm down and gives those tiny injection channels time to close. After a couple of days, the extra risk of bruising or swelling from alcohol is much lower.

Other Ways to Help Your Recovery

Avoiding alcohol helps a lot, but a few other habits can support healing too. Staying well hydrated with water is at the top of the list. It supports your skin and can help with swelling.

It is also smart to skip hard workouts, saunas, and steam rooms for the first day. A big jump in heart rate and heavy heat on your face can raise blood flow and make puffiness worse. Sleeping with your head on an extra pillow the first night can help fluid drain a bit away from the face.

A little planning here is worth it. Skipping drinks for a few days trades a short pause on alcohol for less swelling, less bruising, and a clearer look at your filler results sooner.
